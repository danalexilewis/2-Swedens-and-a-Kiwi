# Sweden — rental stock scale (wave 1)

## Findings (numbered, most load-bearing first)

1. **~2.05 million hyresrätter nationally; allmännytta is ~41%, private landlords ~59%.** SCB/Boverket (2024–25): ~2,051,000 hyreslägenheter total; allmännyttigt bestånd ~843,000–846,000 — “knappt 41 procent av hyresrättsbeståndet” and “knappt 16 procent av det totala bostadsbeståndet.” Sveriges Allmännytta (2024): members own ~847,000 of ~2,051,000 hyreslägenheter (“nästan en miljon”). Private share is growing: SCB added ~16,400 hyresrätter in 2025 vs allmännyttan +4,100.

2. **Municipal public rental is large but no longer dominant within hyresrätt.** Boverket: allmännyttan’s share of total hyresrätt stock is falling even as absolute stock rises. Metro split (allmännytta share of local hyres+private rental): Storgöteborg ~50%, Storstockholm ~40%, Stormalmö ~⅓; some municipalities 80–85%, others none.

3. **First-hand allocation is queue-gated at scale; Stockholm and Göteborg queues are enormous.** Bostadsförmedlingen (31 Dec 2025): **894,592** registered; avg kötid for vanliga hyresrätter **9.0 years** (Stockholms stad **12.4**; innerstad up to **21** per agency). Mediated **13,257** ordinary first-hand contracts in 2025 (~20,861 total incl. student/ungdom/senior). Boplats Väst (31 Dec 2025): **307,593** in queue; avg **2,493 ködagar** (~6.8 years) for mediated contracts; **6,853** lägenheter mediated (89% allmännyttiga).

4. **National first-hand vs andrahand stock is not officially counted.** SCB housing stock is by upplåtelseform/ägarkategori, not first/second-hand occupancy. No national register of andrahandsuthyrning. Best anchors: SCB ~**1%** of population in andrahand nationally (2011); Länsstyrelsen Stockholm ~**3%** in andra/tredje hand in länet (~49,000 people, 2011 survey). Boverket BME (1 Jan 2025): ~**15,000** kommunal social andrahand units + ~**5,700** without biståndsbeslut — welfare-sector only, not total market.

5. **Secondary/gray market is material but statistically invisible.** Länsstyrelsen (Stockholm landlords, ~2012): licensed andrahand tiny vs landlord estimates of **10–20%** olovlig andrahand in major municipal portfolios. Chapter implication: scarcity of *accessible first-hand* stock is measurable via queues; andrahand is a parallel market whose scale must be inferred, not cited precisely.

6. **Vacancy is low; “empty stock” is not the binding constraint.** SCB (1 Sep 2024): **41,314** outhyrda hyreslägenheter; **20,246** lediga för omedelbar uthyrning = **1.3%** of all hyreslägenheter.

## Options / forks this opens

- **Parent vs child frame:** ~840k allmännytta units with day-one besittningsskydd vs ~1.2m private first-hand hyresrätter (same law) — but queue access concentrates perceived “real” tenure in municipal/priority channels.
- **Regional fork:** Göteborg still ~half allmännytta locally (Boverket); Stockholm/Malmö more private — author’s Poseidon/Göteborg lens is atypical nationally.
- **Scarcity metric fork:** queue years (Stockholm ~9, Göteborg ~7) vs vacancy (1.3%) — stock exists; *first-hand access* is the bottleneck.
- **Andrahand fork:** cite welfare-sector 20,700 (BME) vs survey ~1–3% population — or landlord illegal-uthyrning estimates; each supports “children priced out” with different epistemic weight.
