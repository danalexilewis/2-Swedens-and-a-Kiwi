## Göteborg / Poseidon local share vs national ~41%

**National baseline:** Allmännytta is a large minority of hyresrätt (~41%), not the majority. Private landlords hold most hyresrätter (~59%) and can grant the *same* first-hand tenure law (see S10 note).

**Author’s lived lens is atypical upward:**
- **Storgöteborg:** allmännytta owns **about half** of the local hyres market — highest of the three metro areas (Boverket).
- **Framtiden** (~76k units) is Sweden’s largest municipal housing group; Poseidon alone (~29k) is the city’s largest single landlord.
- Poseidon/Framtiden self-describe: Poseidon houses ~every **tenth** Gothenburger; Framtiden group ~every **fourth** — institutional colour, not a substitute for the Boverket share.
- Rough scale check (illustrative, not a new official %): if Göteborg has on the order of ~310k dwellings and ~half are hyresrätt, Framtiden’s ~76k is roughly **half of local hyresrätt** — consistent with Boverket’s Storgöteborg “närmare hälften,” and far denser than the national 41% of hyresrätt / 16% of all dwellings.
- **Storstockholm** (~40%) and especially **Stormalmö** (~⅓) are thinner municipal shares; some municipalities have no allmännytta at all, others 80–85%.

**Chapter implication:** Poseidon/Guldheden is evidence of a *working local institution at high density*, not proof that Sweden already delivers that density nationally. The expand case is: keep the tenure model; grow accessible first-hand stock where queues show rationing.

### Plain-language summary

Sweden has a large **hyresrätt** (purpose-built rental) stock — about **2.05 million** dwellings. Roughly **two-fifths** (~41%, ~846,000 units by end-2025) sit in **allmännytta** (municipal housing companies); about **three-fifths** are privately owned. The municipal share of *all* Swedish dwellings is only about **16%**. Absolute allmännytta stock is still growing, but private landlords are adding faster, so the municipal *share of hyresrätt* is falling.

That stock is not “empty and unused.” On 1 September 2024 only **1.3%** of hyreslägenheter were vacant and immediately available to let. The binding constraint is **access to first-hand contracts**, measured by public queues: Stockholm’s agency had **~895,000** registered and an average **nine years** wait for ordinary contracts in 2025 (city **12.4**, inner city ~**21**); Göteborg’s Boplats had **~308,000** registered and average waits of about **seven years**, with most mediated units from allmännytta.

Göteborg is a **high-allmännytta** metro (Storgöteborg ~half of local rental vs national ~41% of hyresrätt). Framtiden (~76,000 units) and Poseidon (~29,000) concentrate that model; they are not the national average. Private first-hand hyresrätt carries the same tenure law as municipal first-hand — queues and allocation, not a different legal species, create the scarcity children feel.

**Andrahand** is a parallel residual market. There is no trustworthy national headcount of second-hand occupancy in the dwelling register. Cite queues and vacancy for the scarcity claim; treat andrahand scale as qualitative / survey / welfare-sector only.

### Useful facts (chapter-safe)

1. ~2.05m hyresrätter; allmännytta ~41% / ~16% of all dwellings; private ~59% of hyresrätt.  
2. Queues = rationing of *access*, not proof of empty stock (vacancy 1.3%).  
3. Stockholm ~9 years avg / Göteborg ~7 years avg (2025 mediated contracts).  
4. Author’s Poseidon city is denser municipal rental than Sweden overall — expand case, not “already solved nationally.”  
5. Do not equate “private hyresrätt” with “andrahand”; private owners grant first-hand too.

### Claims it supports

- Chapter fact #4: scale of secure municipal rental vs private rental stock; scarcity as access failure.  
- “Sweden should expand what it has” — institution exists at scale but is rationed and regionally uneven.  
- Does **not** support: “only allmännytta has besittningsskydd”; “X% of Swedes live in andrahand nationally” as a precise stock claim.

### Limitations

- Queue registration ≠ active searchers (Stockholm: only ~10% made ≥5 interest applications in 2025).  
- Not all first-hand lettings pass through Boplats / Bostadsförmedlingen (direct landlord channels exist; BME: seeking directly at the company is the most common path nationally).  
- Poseidon apartment counts drift slightly across company pages vs older annual reports — cite year.  
- Andrahand national scale remains epistemically weak by design of registers.

### One-line chapter answer

**Sweden already has ~2 million hyresrätter and ~840–850k allmännytta units (~41% of rental / ~16% of all homes), but metro queues of ~7–9 years (Stockholm city higher; inner city ~21) against 1.3% vacancy show the failure is scarce *access* to first-hand contracts — Göteborg/Poseidon is a high-density local version of that model, not the national average; do not invent a national andrahand stock count.**
