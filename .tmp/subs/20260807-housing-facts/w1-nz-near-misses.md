## Findings (numbered, most load-bearing first)

1. **NZ’s largest “public rental” stock is need-gated welfare housing, not open modest rental.** ~87,000 IRRS-funded places (Kāinga Ora ~73,000; CHPs ~14,000) but access requires MSD assessment of “serious housing need” — current housing, ability to “afford…access…and sustain a private rental” — plus income/assets tests ($832.73/week single; $1,281.12 with partner/children; cash assets &lt;$42,700). Not a Poseidon-like on-ramp for studying/saving youth without parental housing.

2. **Scale exists but is thin vs population and peers.** Te Waihanga (Nov 2025): social housing ≈3.8% of NZ housing stock; 14.4 dwellings per 1,000 people vs OECD average 32.7. Kāinga Ora houses ~200,000 people — large landlord, small share of membership.

3. **Queue and triage contradict “secure modest rental at scale.”** Housing Register: 19,002 applicants (MSD, 30 Jun 2026). HUD quarterly reports: median ~182–206 days to house those placed (Sep–Dec 2023); Priority A (“at risk”) ~93% of register (HUD Cabinet paper 2023). Waiting is explicitly priority-by-urgency, not first-come workforce housing.

4. **IRRS reinforces welfare function, not ladder function.** Rent generally capped at 25% of net income; HUD pays provider the gap to market rent. MSD: IRRS only for clients referred from the public housing register — providers cannot claim it for tenants housed under their own criteria. Surplus recycles via Crown/CHP governance, not tenant membership.

5. **Tenure is RTA landlord-tenant, not Nordic first-hand security.** Public housing tenancies are under Residential Tenancies Act 1986; Kāinga Ora may terminate/transfer (e.g. s53B relocation; s55A disruptive behaviour). Continued eligibility can end when MSD determines housing need no longer meets thresholds (Ministerial Direction on Continued Eligibility).

6. **Housing NZ legacy: state housing was once broader working-class provision; policy narrowed it to “most needy.”** NZ History / Te Ara: National 1990s market rents + sales; stock fell sharply (Motu: major sell-off 1990s, rebuild 2000s). Te Waihanga: register more than quadrupled 2017–2022 — demand for residual housing, not expanded ladder stock.

7. **CHPs mirror Kāinga Ora on access; differ on ownership/reinvestment.** ~61 registered providers, ~14,000 IRRS places (Te Waihanga). CHA Hub: sector ~19,700 units incl. transitional/affordable — still MSD-matched for IRRS social housing. Not-for-profit reinvestment is structurally closer to allmännytta; gatekeeping is identical.

8. **Council housing: local near-miss, same national gate.** Wellington ~1,900 homes via Te Toi Mahana CHP (WCC, 2023) — council retains ownership, CHP manages; **new** eligible tenants access IRRS via MSD. Stats NZ via Te Waihanga: council social housing assets ~$4.5b (2022). Not a separate youth/study stream.

9. **Te Kāinga (Wellington) is the clearest functional analogue — but tiny and city-only.** Council landlord; “long-term, stable rental agreements” for workers who don’t own homes; ~180 apartments (Feb 2023), target 1,500 by 2033. Market-affordable (not IRRS); priority sectors, not open modest rental at national scale.

10. **HUD “affordable rentals” still target high need, not general modest security.** Income/asset thresholds match social housing; tenants must show barriers beyond affordability; annual reviews remove households who exceed thresholds or no longer have “high housing need.”

## Options / forks this opens

- **Rebrand vs rebuild:** ~87k homes already deliver secure rental for some; the gap is eligibility design (need/SAS) not just stock count.
- **Te Kāinga as template:** workforce-targeted, council-landlord, long-term leases — could scale if detached from IRRS/welfare queue; currently Wellington-only and &lt;200 units.
- **CHP/allmännytta hybrid:** not-for-profit reinvestment without MSD triage — would require new tenure product and subsidy line.
- **Historical fork:** pre-1990s state housing as broader class provision vs post-reform “residual” model — policy choice, not market inevitability.
