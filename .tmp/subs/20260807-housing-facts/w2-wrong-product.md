# Wave 2 — Wrong product (NZ near-misses / fact #5)

**Option:** Lead with WRONG PRODUCT. Kāinga Ora / IRRS / CHPs are need-gated welfare housing — not a Poseidon/hyresrätt-like starter for middle-class or ordinary young people. NZ needs a product for middle-class NZ and specifically for young people to start adult life with security.

**Wave-1 base:** `.tmp/subs/20260807-housing-facts/w1-nz-near-misses.md`  
**Chapter force:** NZ copy Poseidon *function*; Sweden expand — `topics/institutions-worth-keeping.md`, `chapters/what-made-them-rich-made-their-children-poor.md`  
**Retrieved / deepened:** 2026-08-07

---

## Recommendation for this option

**Lead the NZ near-miss spine with wrong product, not thin stock.** NZ already has a large public landlord (~87k IRRS places; Kāinga Ora ~73k; CHPs ~14k) and still lacks the Poseidon *function*: open-enough, secure, modest rental that ordinary young people and middle-income non-owners can use to study, save, and start adult life without parental co-residence or an MSD “serious housing need” triage.

Stock-thinness (social housing ≈3.8% of dwellings; 14.4 per 1,000 vs OECD 32.7) is true and useful as colour — but the load-bearing claim is **eligibility design**. Expanding residual welfare housing does not create a youth/middle-class starter product. Te Kāinga proves the right *product class* exists in NZ; it is still **wrong scale** (city-only; hundreds of units vs city-scale Poseidon tens of thousands).

---

## How it works / what it takes (chapter logic)

1. **Name the product NZ has:** IRRS social housing — income/asset-tested; MSD Social Allocation System; serious housing need; Priority A–dominated register; rent ≈25% of net income with Crown paying the market gap.
2. **Name the product NZ needs:** Poseidon-function — secure modest rental at scale; provider earns a return; surplus reinvested in housing; tenants can plan without proving crisis or residual need.
3. **Show the near-misses:** CHPs and council stock mostly share the IRRS gate; historical state housing once served a broader working class and was narrowed to “most needy.”
4. **Show the tiny right product:** Te Kāinga — council-managed, long-term stable leases, market-affordable (not IRRS), income bands that explicitly include people *not* eligible for income-related rent.
5. **Youth start path (labelled interpretation):** halls → private-rental gauntlet → no security → further marginalisation — explains *why* the missing product matters without inventing hall enrolment stats.

## Te Kāinga — tiny right product / wrong scale (updated)

Wave 1 said ~180 apartments (Feb 2023). **Update — still accurate as “right product, wrong scale,” but count has grown:**

- **Product:** Wellington City Council affordable rental programme; “long-term, stable rental agreements”; warm/dry; council tenancy management; partnership with private building owners (office-to-apartment). Aimed at medium-to-lower incomes who struggle to access quality city housing. **Not IRRS.**
- **Eligibility / priority:** Anyone can apply; affordability check. Under high demand: prioritise incomes under **$95,000** (individual) / **$150,000** (couple/family), **not eligible for income-related rent**, non-owners, plus priority work sectors (public sector, health, education, hospitality, etc.) then ballot.
- **Scale (WCC project page, retrieved 2026-08-07):** **472** apartments in the Te Kāinga programme — 292 managed by Te Kāinga team, 78 subleased to Victoria University, 102 supporting council social-housing upgrade. Target: **1,500 by 2033** (raised May 2025). Dec 2025 Te Puna opening news cited 370 after that building — use the live WCC programme total and footnote if numbers move.
- **Contrast:** Poseidon alone ≈28,876 hyresrätter in Göteborg (`sources/notes/sweden-first-hand-tenure-allmannytta.md`). Te Kāinga is the clearest NZ functional analogue and still **city-only and two orders of magnitude smaller** than city-scale allmännytta.

**Chapter use:** “We almost invented the product. We did not invent the scale.”

## Youth start path — AUTHOR INTERPRETATION (do not invent hall stats)

**Label in manuscript:** interpretation / personal opinion — not a statistical claim about enrolment, halls capacity, or tenancy outcomes.

**Operator force to preserve:**

1. **University halls are a terrible model for adult membership.** Students do not learn civil behaviour or societal participation; they live in a strange bubble — kind of beautiful, but not good enough as a civic start.
2. **After halls: a gauntlet.** Navigating poor-quality private rentals with agents and landlords who hold too much power.
3. **No security for the young means further marginalisation.** Without a secure modest base, youth cannot study, save, take risk, or belong as full economic members.

**How to use with facts (without inventing numbers):**  
Pair the interpretation with the *sourced* absence of a non-welfare starter product. The halls → gauntlet story explains the lived path into the private market; the IRRS/eligibility facts explain why the big public landlord is not the exit from that gauntlet for ordinary young people. Do **not** cite invented hall-enrolment percentages, average halls years, or “X% of students…” lines.

**Link to Poseidon force:** Secure modest rental at scale is the missing third path — neither parental home, nor residual welfare triage, nor insecure private rental.

## Plain-language summary

New Zealand’s largest “public rental” system is **income-related rent subsidy (IRRS) social housing**, delivered mainly by Kāinga Ora and registered community housing providers. Access requires MSD assessment of **serious housing need** plus income and asset tests. Allocation runs through the Housing Register and Social Allocation System. Tenant rent is generally capped at about **25% of net income**; the Crown pays providers the gap to market rent.

That is a **welfare / residual-need product**. It is not an open, city-scale secure modest rental for ordinary young people or middle-income non-owners who can afford private rent in theory but lack security, quality, or a place to start adult life without parental housing.

**Te Kāinga** (Wellington) is closer to the missing function: council-managed, long-term stable leases, not IRRS, with priority bands that include people not eligible for income-related rent. It remains tiny and Wellington-only relative to national need and to Swedish municipal landlords such as Poseidon.

## Useful facts

- ~87,000 IRRS / government-funded social places (KO ~73k; CHPs ~14k) — Te Waihanga, Nov 2025.
- Social housing ≈3.8% of stock; 14.4 per 1,000 people vs OECD 32.7 — Te Waihanga.
- Eligibility: serious housing need + income/asset tests (MSD; figures time-sensitive).
- IRRS: ~25% of net income; HUD pays market gap — HUD.
- Te Kāinga: programme ~472 apartments (WCC page); target 1,500 by 2033; priority incomes under $95k/$150k and not IRRS-eligible — tekainga-apartments.nz / WCC.
- Poseidon contrast: ~28,876 apartments — S10.

## Claims it supports

- Chapter fact #5: NZ near-misses fall short of Poseidon *function* primarily by **product design (need gate)**, secondarily by scale.
- Proposal: NZ should copy the Poseidon function (secure modest rental at scale), not rebrand IRRS as that function.
- Te Kāinga = existence proof of right product class; wrong scale / wrong geography for national membership.

## Limitations

- Do not claim KO/CHP tenants lack all security — many have real homes; the claim is about *who the product is for*.
- Do not invent youth share of Housing Register or halls statistics.
- Te Kāinga unit counts move as buildings open; re-check WCC page before locking a number.
- RTA tenancy rights ≠ Swedish first-hand besittningsskydd package (separate tenure-map workstream).
- Historical “broader state housing” claim needs careful NZ History / Motu wording if used as more than colour.

## Short “youth start path” block (chapter-ready)

*[interpretation / personal opinion — not statistics]*

New Zealand does not give ordinary young people a civic housing start. University halls are a beautiful bubble that teaches almost nothing about living as a member of a society — civil behaviour, neighbours, contribution. After that comes the gauntlet: shit rentals, agents, landlords with too much power. There is no secure modest rung between parental home, residual welfare housing, and provisional private rent. Without that rung, the young are further marginalised — asked to study, save, and take risk while standing on ice.

*[sourced hinge]* What New Zealand *does* have at national scale — Kāinga Ora, IRRS, community housing — is need-gated welfare housing for serious housing need. That is a necessary product for some. It is the wrong product for the Poseidon function. Wellington’s Te Kāinga points at the right product and proves how small it still is.

---

## Risks and failure modes

- **Rebrand trap:** Readers hear “expand Kāinga Ora” and miss the eligibility redesign. Chapter must keep “wrong product” as the lead, stock thinness as secondary.
- **Insulting social tenants:** Critique product design, not the people housed. Welfare housing is real security for those who get it.
- **Te Kāinga overclaim:** It is workforce/city-targeted and priority-balloted under demand — not fully open allmännytta. Call it closest analogue, not identity.
- **Halls overclaim:** Keep as labelled interpretation; no fake enrolment stats; do not universalise every student’s experience.
- **Tenure conflation:** Even a new NZ product must specify security of tenure separately (RTA vs hyresrätt) — see tenure-map wave.
- **Political misread:** “Middle-class housing” can sound like diverting scarce social stock. Frame as *additional product line / capability*, not raiding the register.

---

## What would change the recommendation

- Evidence of a **national** non-IRRS secure modest rental product at meaningful scale (thousands+ per major city, open or lightly queued without SAS residual triage).
- Te Kāinga (or peers) scaling to **city-defining** stock with durable tenure and reinvestment rules — then the claim shifts from “missing product” to “under-scaled product.”
- Register/eligibility redesign that creates a genuine youth/study/work starter stream *without* serious-housing-need gate — then “wrong product” softens to “wrong allocation inside one landlord.”
- Hard data that most under-30s already access long secure public rental without parental help — would undercut the youth-path force (none found in wave 1; do not invent).
