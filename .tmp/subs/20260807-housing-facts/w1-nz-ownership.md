## Findings (numbered, most load-bearing first)

1. **Young-adult ownership collapsed while national rates fell only modestly.** HES (Stats NZ): share of 25–34-year-olds who are owner-occupiers fell from ~65% (1988) to ~35% (2018), excluding people living with parents (Bentley 2019, citing HES). Census: people aged 25–29 *living in* an owner-occupied dwelling fell from 61% (1991) to 43.7% (2018); 35–39 from 79.8% to 59.2% (Stats NZ 2020 news release).

2. **Headline household ownership masks generational stress.** Household tenure peaked at 73.8% (1991), fell to 64.5% (2018), ticked up to 66.0% (2023) — includes full/part ownership and family trust (Stats NZ 2023 census highlights). Stats NZ notes 2023 gains skew to older, higher-home-ownership areas (2024 news release).

3. **Cohort comparison: same age, worse odds.** At 40–44, individual ownership fell from 69% (2001) to 58% (2013) (2018 Housing Stocktake, MBIE/Beehive). Partnered 30–34-year-olds saw the largest 2001–2018 drop in *individual* ownership (−12.4 pp) (Housing in Aotearoa: 2020).

4. **More young adults stay in the parental home.** Census “child in family nucleus”: 18-year-olds living with parent(s) rose 64.6% (2001) → 77.7% (2018); ~32% of Pacific peoples aged 25–29 still in parental home vs ~16% European (Housing in Aotearoa: 2020).

5. **Ownership concentrates wealth across generations.** HES 2018: homeowners (own/part-own) typically **14× wealthier** than non-owners (Housing in Aotearoa: 2020). Treasury: rising prices widen inequality between those on the “wealth ladder” and those not; younger people more likely renters in bottom half of wealth distribution (AN 21/01; Stephens speech 2024 cites under-35 vs over-65 wealth gap widening 2001–2018).

6. **“Bank of Mum and Dad” — no official measure; surveys show scale.** Consumer NZ (2022, n=394 helpers/2,810): **14% of families** financially supported adult children to buy; average **$108,000**; **61%** helped with deposit; **~60%** expected no repayment (consumer.org.nz). Kiwibank-commissioned survey (n=2,008): **34%** of homeowners received family financial help for first home (NZ Herald report). Wang et al. (peer-reviewed, 582 parents 40+): average financial contribution **NZ$260,344** among supporters — different sample/method than Consumer (Lincoln/Taylor & Francis 2025).

7. **Non-financial parental help is structurally important.** Wang et al. distinguish gifts, loans, guarantees and **co-residence to save deposits** (2025). Kāinga Ora First Home Loan allows **5% deposits** including gifts with lender gift declaration (kaingaora.govt.nz). FMA: **$1.8b** withdrawn by **42,811** members for first homes in year to Mar 2025 (avg ~$41k) — state-facilitated deposit pathway, not parental (FMA KiwiSaver Annual Report 2025).

8. **Deposit constraint visible in RBNZ lending.** RBNZ C31 tracks first-home-buyer mortgages monthly; low-equity (LVR>80%) share of new FHB lending has risen sharply (rbnz.govt.nz/statistics — parental sources not recorded).
