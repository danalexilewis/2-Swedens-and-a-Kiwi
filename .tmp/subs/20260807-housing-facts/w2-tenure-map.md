## 1. Tenure map (concepts — not fake equivalence)

| Function for the adult | Sweden concept | What it actually is | Closest NZ form | Mapping note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Own the dwelling outright / freehold-like | **Äganderätt** | Privately owned dwelling (often småhus); household owns title | Freehold / fee simple; also dwelling held in family trust with owner-occupiers | **Good functional match** for “own the house.” NZ census “owned with or without mortgage / family trust” ≈ this bucket for headline rates. |
| Own a share / membership right in multi-unit housing | **Bostadsrätt** | Tenant-ownership cooperative: buy the right to occupy; association owns the building; monthly avgift; capital market for the share | Unit title / cross-lease / company-share apartments; body corporate | **Partial match only.** Both require deposit/mortgage capital and can compound wealth. Bostadsrätt is not freehold title of the unit in the English sense; NZ unit title is closer to äganderätt of a stratum. Do **not** call NZ apartments “bostadsrätt.” |
| Secure, first-hand rental as normal adult housing | **Hyresrätt** (förstahand) | Direct lease with owner; day-one **besittningsskydd**; use-value / negotiated rent; private *or* allmännytta landlord | **No national analogue** | **Gap row.** NZ private rental is RTA landlord–tenant; from 30 Jan 2025 landlords may end periodic tenancies with **90-day no-cause** notice (RTA Amendment Act 2024 / Tenancy Services). That is the opposite of day-one besittningsskydd. |
| Residual / secondary / provisional renting | **Andrahand / privatuthyrning** | Weaker or no besittningsskydd; stopgap when first-hand unavailable | Fixed-term private rentals; flatting; boarding; “waiting for next lease” | **Asymmetric.** Sweden’s failure mode is falling *into* secondary when first-hand is scarce. NZ’s *ordinary* private rental already sits closer to that insecure pole — there is no strong first-hand tier above it. |
| Need-gated public / social rental | **Allmännytta** (institutional form) *delivering* hyresrätt | Municipal company; housing-supply mission; same hyresrätt law; not means-tested as “welfare residual” in the NZ IRRS sense | **Kāinga Ora / CHP IRRS public housing** | **Wrong-product near-miss** (detail elsewhere). Same surface: “state rental.” Different function: SE allmännytta = secure first-hand at city scale for ordinary households; NZ public housing = MSD serious-need gate + income/assets + continued-eligibility review. Te Kāinga (Wellington) is a tiny workforce-rental near-analogue — not national hyresrätt. |

### How to read the map

- Sweden’s triad is **three legitimate adult foundations**. Young adults cluster in flerbostadshus **hyresrätt** and **bostadsrätt**, not småhus äganderätt (SCB age tables — see w1-se-ownership).
- NZ’s lived triad is closer to: **own (or trust)** / **insecure private rent** / **need-gated public rent** — plus **stay with parents**. Missing is the middle Swedish cell: secure non-owned housing that is not residual welfare.
- Do not equate “NZ renter share” with “hyresrätt share.” The labels share “rent”; the security and cultural legitimacy do not.

### Metric fork for the chapter (pick and stay consistent)

| Metric | Use for | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NZ household tenure (owned + trust) | National “ownership culture” headline | Masks young-adult collapse (66% 2023 vs ~35% owner-occupiers aged 25–34 in 2018 HES) |
| NZ “living in owner-occupied dwelling” by age | Dwelling-level period rates | Includes adult children in parental home — **overstates** young ownership |
| NZ individual ownership / title / trust by age | Cohort “who owns” story | Prefer Stocktake / Housing in Aotearoa individual series |
| SE SCB boendeform × age | Who lives how | Folkbokförd ≠ owner; bostadsrätt row includes some subletters |
| SE first-hand hyresrätt among young (survey) | Exit-from-parents path | Hyresgästföreningen ≠ SCB register; 2025 method change |

## 2. Load-bearing facts usable in chapter (fact #1 spine)

Tag each line: **[fact]** / **[survey]** / **[interpretation]**.

### New Zealand — ownership & cohorts

1. **[fact]** Owner-occupier share of 25–34-year-olds (excl. living with parents): ~**65% (1988) → ~35% (2018)** (HES via Bentley 2019).
2. **[fact]** People aged 25–29 *living in* an owner-occupied dwelling: **61% (1991) → 43.7% (2018)**; ages 35–39: **79.8% → 59.2%** (Stats NZ 2020).
3. **[fact]** Household ownership (incl. trust): peak **73.8% (1991)** → **64.5% (2018)** → **66.0% (2023)**; 2023 gains skew older/higher-ownership areas (Stats NZ).
4. **[fact]** Individual ownership at ages 40–44: **69% (2001) → 58% (2013)** (2018 Housing Stocktake).
5. **[fact]** Partnered 30–34-year-olds: largest 2001–2018 drop in *individual* ownership (−**12.4** pp) (Housing in Aotearoa: 2020).
6. **[fact]** 18-year-olds living with parent(s) (“child in family nucleus”): **64.6% (2001) → 77.7% (2018)**; ~**32%** of Pacific peoples aged 25–29 still in parental home vs ~**16%** European (Housing in Aotearoa: 2020).
7. **[fact]** Homeowners typically **~14×** wealthier than non-owners (HES 2018; Housing in Aotearoa: 2020).

### New Zealand — parental help

8. **[survey]** Consumer NZ (2022): **14%** of families financially supported adult children to buy; avg **$108,000**; **61%** helped with deposit; **~60%** expected no repayment.
9. **[survey]** Kiwibank-commissioned (n≈2,008): **34%** of homeowners received family financial help for first home (media report of survey — cite carefully).
10. **[survey]** Wang et al. (2025): among supporting parents 40+, mean financial contribution **NZ$260,344** — different sample than Consumer; also documents co-residence to save deposits.
11. **[fact]** No official Stats NZ / RBNZ time series of parental gifts/loans for purchase (gap to state openly).

### Sweden — triad presence & young adults

12. **[fact]** National household stock (SCB ~2024/25): ~**38%** småhus äganderätt; ~**29%** flerbostadshus hyresrätt; ~**21%** flerbostadshus bostadsrätt.
13. **[fact]** Ages 25–29 (SCB 2025): **40.7%** flerbostadshus hyresrätt; **26.0%** bostadsrätt; **20.9%** småhus äganderätt — rental/cooperative dominate youth housing, not villa ownership.
14. **[fact]** Single-family ownership at age 35: 1973 cohort ~**38%/41.6%** (m/w) vs 1983 cohort ~**28.9%/32.4%** (Enström et al. 2026) — owned entry weakened even where hyresrätt exists.
15. **[survey]** ICA Banken Pengakollen 2025 (19–29): **41%** of young bostadsägare got parental economic help to buy.
16. **[survey]** Hyresgästföreningen 2025 (20–27): **27%** hold first-hand hyresrätt (most common single category); **21%** live with parents; **90%** of those at home want to leave. Prefer 2017 “among those who left home” split for cleaner exit story: **35.1%** first-hand hyresrätt vs **28.8%** any owned form combined (method-stable).

### Sweden — tenure law (already sourced)

17. **[fact]** Förstahand hyresrätt = day-one besittningsskydd + constrained rent-setting (JB 12 kap.); not exclusive to allmännytta — see `sources/notes/sweden-first-hand-tenure-allmannytta.md`.

### NZ security contrast (for the gap row)

18. **[fact]** From 30 Jan 2025, landlord may terminate a periodic tenancy with **≥90 days’ notice without specific ground** (Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2024; Tenancy Services). Shorter **42-day** notices for owner-move-in / sale-with-vacant-possession / employee housing.

## 3. Hyresrätt-gap argument (careful tagging)

### What is established fact

- Sweden has a **named, legally strong, culturally normal** secure-rental tenure (hyresrätt / förstahand) that young adults actually use at scale when they can get it **[fact]**.
- NZ has **falling young-adult ownership**, rising **parental-home delay**, and measurable **parental purchase help** — i.e. security concentrates via ownership + family capital **[fact / survey]**.
- NZ’s main state rental product is **need-gated** (IRRS / Housing Register), not an open modest adult foundation **[fact — see w1-nz-near-misses]**.
- NZ private rental law does **not** provide day-one indefinite security equivalent to besittningsskydd; no-cause termination returned in 2025 **[fact]**.

### What is interpretation / proposal (chapter spine)

- **[interpretation]** NZ’s deepest housing problem for the next generation is not only high prices; it is the **missing middle cell** of the Swedish triad: secure non-owned housing as a legitimate adult foundation.
- **[interpretation]** Comparing “ownership rates” alone understates the Swedish safety valve and overstates NZ’s private-rental “alternative.”
- **[proposal]** NZ should build a **hyresrätt-like function**: direct landlord relationship, strong security of tenure, modest stock at scale, accessible without proving residual welfare need — Poseidon-like in function, not necessarily in brand.
- **[proposal-adjacent]** Do not claim NZ has “no rental law” or that Swedish queues prove the model is worthless; claim **under-built non-owned security** in both countries, and **absent institutional form** in NZ.

### What not to claim

- That bostadsrätt = NZ apartment ownership.
- That Kāinga Ora = allmännytta / hyresrätt (wrong product).
- That Swedish young people are fine because hyresrätt exists (queues; parental help for bostadsrätt; cohort ownership decline).
- That Consumer/Kiwibank/Wang figures are official population parameters.

## Plain-language summary

Sweden’s housing is a **tenure triad**: äganderätt (own), bostadsrätt (cooperative ownership right), hyresrätt (secure first-hand rent). Young adults disproportionately live in hyresrätt and bostadsrätt, not villa ownership. First-hand hyresrätt carries day-one security of tenure under the Land Code.

New Zealand’s statistics are organised around **owner-occupation vs renting** (plus family trust), not a secure-rental third pillar. Young-adult owner-occupation collapsed over three decades while headline household ownership fell only modestly. Parental financial help and co-residence are large, unofficially measured pathways into ownership. Public housing is need-gated residual provision, not open secure rental.
