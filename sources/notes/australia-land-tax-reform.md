# Source: Australian land-tax reform (Henry Review and ACT)

**Author or institution**  
Australia’s Future Tax System Review (Ken Henry, chair); Australian Capital Territory tax reform programme

**Date**  
Henry Review ~2009–2010; ACT reform began 2012 (20-year programme)

Policy document / official reform programme.

## Plain-language summary

The Henry Review recommended a broad land-tax base, possible increasing marginal rates based on land value per square metre, and a long-term move toward including most land. It proposed replacing stamp duties with more efficient taxes such as land tax and stressed transitional rules. The ACT began a multi-decade reform reducing stamp duty and insurance duty while increasing reliance on a broader general-rates system based on land — designed as a tax-mix change, not a simple overall tax increase.

## Useful facts

- Mainstream (not fringe) case for broadening land tax  
- Stamp duty → recurring land/rates substitution  
- Transition design as central, not optional  

## Claims it supports

- Practical LVT-adjacent reform is often gradual replacement of transaction taxes, not “add a huge tax tomorrow”  
- Land taxation can sit inside official tax-reform agendas  

## Limitations

Australia is comparative evidence, not a third country of the book. ACT outcomes need careful reading (incidence, house prices, political sustainability). Do not present Henry Review recommendations as identical to a pure Georgist single tax.
