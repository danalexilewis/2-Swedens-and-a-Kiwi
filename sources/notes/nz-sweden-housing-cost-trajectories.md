## Plain-language summary

Use **two lenses**. **Lens A (entry ticket):** price-to-income, deposits, bostadsrätt *insats* — the cost of joining ownership. **Lens B (monthly burden):** rent-to-income / housing-cost share — the cost of staying housed without (or before) buying. In both countries the ownership ticket rose faster than incomes for a generation. NZ renters also saw a lasting jump in housing-cost share. Swedish first-hand renters’ median share stayed high but flat; outsider squeeze is access and new-build rents, not a national median rent explosion.

## Useful facts

**NZ — A:** Treasury: price/income stable until ~2003 then “highest on record” by 2006. Stats NZ 2020: Auckland ≈ **11.5×** median household disposable. HUD CHAI 2012–22: deposit affordability **−40%**; prices **+143%** vs disposable income **+46%**.

**NZ — B:** HES: renters spending >30% of income **<20% (1988) → ~45%** within a decade, **>40% by 2019**; Jun 2022 year **1 in 4** renting households >40% of disposable.

**SE — A:** OECD PTI (2015=100): **51.0 (1993) → 113.7 (2021) → 94.4 (2024)**. SCB FASTPI small houses peak **985 (2021)**. New-build bostadsrätt 2024 avg *insats* **~3.66m kr**.

**SE — B:** SCB 2024 median housing-cost share: all **23%**; hyresrätt **~27%** (flat since 2015); bostadsrätt **21%**; ägt småhus **15%→18%**. Negotiated hyresrätt hike **5.0% (2024)** highest since 1993; 3-room avg **7,347→8,641 kr/mo (2020–24)**; new-build 3-room **12,177**.
