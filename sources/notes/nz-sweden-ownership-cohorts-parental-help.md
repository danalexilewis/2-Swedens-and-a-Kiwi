## Plain-language summary

Sweden’s housing is a **tenure triad**: äganderätt (own), bostadsrätt (cooperative ownership right), hyresrätt (secure first-hand rent). Young adults disproportionately live in hyresrätt and bostadsrätt. New Zealand’s statistics are organised around **owner-occupation vs renting** (plus family trust). Young-adult owner-occupation collapsed over three decades while headline household ownership fell only modestly. Parental financial help is large and unofficially measured. NZ lacks a hyresrätt-like cell as a normal adult foundation.

## Useful facts

- NZ HES: owner-occupiers aged 25–34 (excl. living with parents) ~**65% (1988) → ~35% (2018)** (Bentley citing HES).
- NZ Census: ages 25–29 *living in* owner-occupied dwelling **61% (1991) → 43.7% (2018)**.
- NZ household ownership (incl. trust): **73.8% (1991) → 64.5% (2018) → 66.0% (2023)** — masks youth stress.
- NZ Stocktake: individual ownership ages 40–44 **69% (2001) → 58% (2013)**.
- Parental help (surveys, not registers): Consumer NZ 2022 — **14%** of families helped adult children buy (avg ~$108k); ICA SE 2025 — **41%** of young buyers got parental help.
- SE SCB ages 25–29: **40.7%** flerbostadshus hyresrätt; **26.0%** bostadsrätt; **20.9%** småhus äganderätt.
- SE cohort: single-family ownership at 35 fell from 1973 → 1983 cohorts (Enström et al. 2026).
- NZ RTA from 30 Jan 2025: landlords may end periodic tenancies with **≥90 days’ no-cause** notice — contrast with Swedish day-one *besittningsskydd*.

## Limitations

- Do not use “living in owner-occupied” as individual ownership (includes parental home).
- Do not average incompatible parental-help surveys.
- Do not equate NZ renter % with hyresrätt %.
- Hyresgästföreningen 2025 method change — prefer stable SCB / earlier exit splits where comparing years.
