## Plain-language summary

Anderson (*Private Government*) frames workplaces as private governments: employees subject to sanctioning authorities with limited accountability — philosophical ground for why escaping a human manager can feel like freedom. Rosenblat (*Uberland*) shows the algorithmic boss: removing the visible manager embeds power in software, pricing, ratings, allocation, and asymmetries. Prassl (*Humans as a Service*) and Aloisi & De Stefano (*Your Boss Is an Algorithm*) analyse platform promises of autonomy against surveillance and precariousness. Moore (*The Quantified Self in Precarity*) links continuous measurement to stress and precarity.

## Useful facts

- Private government as workplace frame (Anderson)  
- Algorithmic boss without eliminating managerial power (Rosenblat)  
- Flexibility vs employment protections tension (Prassl; Aloisi & De Stefano)  
- Quantification / surveillance psychology (Moore)  

## Claims it supports

- Attraction to machine authority can come from intolerable human authority  
- Removing the human manager does not remove power; it can hide it  
- Platform flexibility can coexist with algorithmic control
