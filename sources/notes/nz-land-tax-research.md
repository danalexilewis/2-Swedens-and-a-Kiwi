## Plain-language summary

Mainstream New Zealand economists have modelled land versus capital-value rating, distributional effects, and local-government finance. Coleman and Grimes describe land taxation as relatively efficient because land is immobile, while taxing total capital value also taxes improvements. Grimes, Aitken, and Kerr found land taxes more progressive than capital-value taxes in the local systems they examined. Gemmell and Grimes studied Auckland rating-system changes and found little short-term evidence of effects on new building, with stronger evidence on alterations.

## Useful facts

- Land tax efficiency arguments (immobile base)  
- Progressivity comparisons: land-value vs capital-value rates  
- Auckland quasi-natural experiment: limited short-term new-build effect; stronger alteration evidence  

## Claims it supports

- Taxing total property value can discourage improvements more than taxing land value alone (theory + mixed empirics)  
- Do not overclaim a NZ construction boom from land-based rating
