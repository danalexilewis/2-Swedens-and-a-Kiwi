## Plain-language summary

IE 2021 reported substantial European support for replacing some MPs with algorithms — later work notes the question confounded AI introduction with reducing the number of politicians. German work finds more caution about high-level AI governing authority; political dissatisfaction may not strongly explain enthusiasm. A 2026 UK study (~1,500) reported ~69.2% preferring shared decision-making among politicians, citizens, experts, and/or AI rather than one dominant actor. Hidalgo’s Augmented Democracy proposes personalized AI representatives; Tang & Weyl’s *Plurality* emphasises technology for human collective intelligence; Noveck’s *Reboot* argues AI can be designed to strengthen democratic institutions. Verify all figures and titles before load-bearing use.

## Useful facts

- Survey wording matters (AI + fewer politicians confound)  
- Shared multi-actor preference may be more important than “replace MPs” headlines  
- Distinct proposals: AI representative vs plural collective intelligence vs institutional reboot  

## Claims it supports

- Machine roles in government no longer sound absurd — without proving people want machine rule  
- Hybrid / shared authority is a live public preference candidate  

## Limitations

Surveys are fragile to wording; national contexts differ; TED/popular books are not empirical proof of Swedish or NZ attitudes. Do not invent poll numbers.
