## Plain-language summary

Arnott and Stiglitz formalized a relationship between public expenditure and aggregate land rents (the Henry George theorem). It does not establish that every public project exactly pays for itself through land value, but it gives a formal basis for the idea that public goods are reflected in land rents. The OECD describes land-value capture as recovering some land-value increase from infrastructure and administrative actions such as rezoning. The Lincoln Institute provides balanced research on implementation, valuation, international cases, and criticism as well as support. Stiglitz’s broader work on rent extraction, land and wealth inequality, and non-produced assets supports treating land and rents as central to inequality without reducing him to a Georgist writer.

## Useful facts

- Henry George theorem: public goods ↔ land rents (with stated conditions)  
- OECD land-value capture framing  
- Lincoln: valuation, legal, revenue, and distributional implementation questions  

## Claims it supports

- Public infrastructure and planning create land-value gains often captured by existing owners  
- Formal / mainstream support for reclaiming socially created location value  

## Limitations

Theorem ≠ every project pays for itself. OECD and Lincoln material must be read for scope and country coverage. Specific NZ/Swedish examples still need local evidence. Stiglitz is broader than Georgism.
