## Plain-language summary

George asked why material progress could coexist with continued poverty and insecurity. His answer focused heavily on land rents: population growth, infrastructure, business activity, and technological progress make locations more valuable, allowing landowners to capture an increasing share of the benefit. He argued for taxing land rather than work and produced capital.

## Useful facts

- Foundational statement of the land-rent diagnosis  
- Distinction between produced capital and land  
- Original case for taxing land rather than labour  

## Claims it supports

- Public investment / community activity raises land rents captured by owners  
- Productive activity vs passive capture of scarcity value (philosophical foundation)  

## Limitations

Philosophical foundation, not a modern tax code. Do **not** assume every part of the nineteenth-century single-tax proposal applies unchanged today. Not empirical NZ/Sweden evidence.
