# Argument Map

This shows how the major claims connect. It is an argument map, not a claim that every arrow is already conclusively proven. Each connection becomes something the book explains, supports, challenges, and qualifies.

## Central Outcome

Unconstrained intergenerational inequality.

## Political and Economic Management Failure

Sweden and New Zealand protected incumbent asset values and short-term stability without adequately managing the long-term distributional consequences.

## Main Mechanisms

1. Housing scarcity
2. Credit and monetary policy
3. Tax-preferred asset accumulation
4. Inheritance
5. Political power of incumbent owners
6. Weak alternatives for building security

## Human Consequences

1. Delayed homeownership
2. Rental insecurity
3. Reduced family formation
4. Lower entrepreneurial risk-taking
5. Reduced productivity
6. Youth alienation
7. Brain drain
8. Declining trust in political institutions

## Destination

A capital-class society in which family ownership increasingly determines life chances.

## Trajectory (working argument chain)

```
Housing and financial assets rise faster than wages
            ↓
Existing owners gain wealth and borrowing power
            ↓
Tax advantages amplify the gains
            ↓
Wealth is transferred through families
            ↓
Housing security becomes increasingly inherited
            ↓
People without family capital delay ownership, children, and risk-taking
            ↓
Entrepreneurship and productivity suffer
            ↓
Young people leave for places with better opportunities
            ↓
Political power becomes increasingly aligned with older asset owners
            ↓
The system becomes even harder to reform
```

These are working arguments and predictions. Do not treat every causal connection as verified fact.

## Destination detail

The death of the broadly accessible home-owning middle class and the emergence of a society divided increasingly by capital ownership.

Income alone becomes insufficient to secure what a middle-class income previously promised:

- A stable home
- Room for children
- Reasonable independence
- The ability to take professional or entrepreneurial risks
- A secure retirement
- A belief that the next generation can establish itself
