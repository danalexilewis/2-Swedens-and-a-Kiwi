# Shared Failure

## Definition

Shared Failure is the recurring theme of the book: political and economic management in Sweden and New Zealand has protected incumbent asset values and short-term stability without adequately managing the long-term distributional consequences.

## What it is not

Shared Failure is not a claim that Sweden and New Zealand are identical, or that every mechanism works the same way in both countries.

It is a claim that similar outcomes have appeared through related but distinct national paths.

## Core formulation

Both countries have allowed wealth advantages to compound and transfer between generations without meaningful limits or sufficient counterweights.

## Fair framing

Some emergency decisions may have been reasonable. Some tradeoffs were genuinely difficult. The shared failure is the repeated failure to correct course once the distributional effects became clear.

## What Shared Failure organizes

Every topic file asks:

- What similar outcome has appeared in both countries?
- What distinct mechanisms produced the New Zealand and Swedish versions?
- What common pattern remains despite the differences?
- Who benefits and who carries the cost?

This keeps the comparison central and prevents two separate national books sharing a cover.

## Working line

The book is an argument against denial in the present.
