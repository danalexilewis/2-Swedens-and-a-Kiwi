# Topic: Productivity and Risk

## Shared Failure

Societies cannot demand innovation while making personal security dependent on avoiding risk.

*(interpretation)*

## Why It Matters

When housing security requires predictable income and large deposits, founders and experimenters are filtered out unless they have family support. Productive investment loses to passive asset holding.

*(causal-argument)*

## New Zealand

- Mortgage eligibility rewarding predictable income
- Scaling problem for companies that do form
- Housing costs suppressing business formation
- Choice between buying a house and funding a company

*(personal-experience + mechanisms)*

## Sweden

- Conformity and employment structures
- Limited ability to experiment when security is tied to conventional paths
- Productive investment versus passive asset holding

*(interpretation + evidence needed)*

## Important Differences

Startup ecosystems, capital markets, and employment protection differ. The shared bind: personal security depends on not taking the risks the economy claims to want.

## Common Mechanism

Mortgage and housing systems reward stable wages; entrepreneurial income looks like risk; families without capital cannot tolerate uncertain income; fewer businesses are founded; productivity suffers.

*(causal-argument)*

## Who Benefits?

Incumbent asset holders; those with family capital who can take risks; economies of passive ownership relative to new entrants.

*(interpretation)*

## Who Carries the Cost?

Would-be founders; workers whose wages depend on a less dynamic economy; countries that lose innovators to places where risk is more affordable.

*(interpretation / prediction-adjacent)*

## Personal Material

The choice between putting deposits toward a house and funding a company. This is a lived scene, not a metaphor.

*(personal-experience)* — see `scenes/SCENE-BANK.md`

## Strongest Counterargument

Most startups fail; discouraging reckless leverage is prudent. Productivity problems have many causes (skills, regulation, market size) beyond housing.

*(interpretation — steelman)*

## My Response

Prudence for banks is not the same as a healthy innovation system. The question is how many businesses never exist because potential founders cannot risk losing access to housing.

*(causal-argument / open research question)*
