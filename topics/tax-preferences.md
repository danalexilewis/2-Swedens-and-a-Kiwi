# Topic: Tax Preferences

## Shared Failure

Both countries offer mechanisms that are formally available to everyone but whose greatest benefits flow to people with the most capital.

*(interpretation / causal-argument)*

## Why It Matters

Formal equality hides unequal capacity. Tax rules that look fair on paper can amplify ownership advantages and accelerate unconstrained intergenerational inequality.

*(interpretation)*

## New Zealand

- Property gains treatment: New Zealand does not apply a comprehensive capital-gains tax to ordinary long-held housing gains *(fact — needs reference)*
- Some sale profits are taxed under intention, bright-line, dealer, developer, or related rules — do **not** say every property gain is untaxed *(fact — needs reference)*
- Council rates tax property; rates are not the same as a general recurring tax on unreclaimed increases in unimproved land value *(interpretation — needs verification)* — see `topics/taxing-activity-rewarding-scarcity.md`
- CGT politics is not uniform hostility: ~43% supported taxing investment-property gains, ~36% opposed, ~22% undecided (2025 RNZ–Reid Research); support falls dramatically when the family home is included *(fact — needs primary poll citation)*
- Family-home exemption is a political distinction dressed as economics: investment-property gains treated as income-like; large owner-occupied gains treated as “family security” *(interpretation)*
- 2019 Tax Working Group recommended extending capital-gains taxation but family home was outside terms of reference; TWG noted the exemption incentivises putting more money into owner-occupied housing *(fact — needs TWG citation)*
- Legitimate CGT design objections exist (inflation, paper gains without cash, realisation, lock-in, losses, founders, farms, inheritance, valuation at start, productive investment) — do not reduce every opponent to self-interest *(interpretation)*
- Distrust of government: “another tax” without naming what is reduced reads as a further claim on savings, not a correction of imbalance *(interpretation)*
- Other capital vs labour treatment to be mapped carefully

## Important Differences

The instruments differ (property-gain treatment vs ISK and related vehicles). Do not treat them as the same policy. Compare the pattern: formal availability, unequal benefit.

## Common Mechanism

Equal access to a tax advantage does not produce equal benefit when access to capital is radically unequal.

Related mechanism: the system observes and taxes productive activity more consistently than it reclaims society-created land value. See `topics/taxing-activity-rewarding-scarcity.md`.

Turn-to-win politics (NZ force, Shared Failure grammar): asset-based inequality recruits some losers into defending the mechanism because they still hope to become winners — “I don’t want to lose my turn to win.” Treat as interpretation, not polling proof.

The issue is not necessarily that these mechanisms exist.

It is that the benefit is insufficiently capped.

*(interpretation / proposal-adjacent)*

## Who Benefits?

Households and owners with substantial capital who can place assets inside preferred treatments; intergenerational winners who compound gains.

*(interpretation)*

## Who Carries the Cost?

Wage earners relative to capital owners; public revenue foregone; non-owners who face higher asset prices partly sustained by preferred after-tax returns.

*(causal-argument — needs care and evidence)*

## Strongest Counterargument

These mechanisms encourage investment, simplify tax administration, and are open to anyone who saves. Caps and new taxes can damage growth and capital formation.

*(interpretation — steelman)*

## My Response

The argument is not that every capital preference must be abolished. It is that uncapped or weakly capped preferences amplify unequal starting capital into permanent class advantage.

Politically stronger Georgist formulation: don’t just tax capital more — change what we tax. More on scarce land, economic rents, and very large inherited advantages; less on wages, productive investment, building, and entrepreneurship. The burden is sitting on the wrong things.

Land tax may suit the book better than a blanket CGT: founding Eddy and selling for NZ$50m is not the same economic origin as an Auckland section rising NZ$5m because the city grew around it.

*(interpretation / proposal-adjacent)*
