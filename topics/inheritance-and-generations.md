# Topic: Inheritance and Generations

## Shared Failure

The wealth created through decades of property and asset inflation is becoming intergenerational power.

Stronger formulation (preserve both): the system became so unequal that inheritance started looking like the solution to the inequality. Underneath: nobody wants to end the game because everyone is waiting for their turn to win.

*(interpretation / causal-argument)*

## Why It Matters

When adult stability depends on family transfers, the social contract becomes an inheritance contract. Timing matters: inheritance often arrives too late for housing and family formation decisions.

The darker generational promise: work hard, rent, wait for your parents to die, inherit their property wealth, and finally acquire security. That is an extraordinary deterioration in the social contract. CGT politics can accidentally reinforce it — an adult child excluded by housing inflation defending untaxed parental gains that caused much of their exclusion (“Don’t tax Dad’s house. That’s how I’m eventually going to afford mine.”).

*(interpretation)*

## Important Differences

Inheritance tax history, family norms, and housing tenure differ. The shared pattern is intergenerational transfer of ownership advantage, not identical legal regimes.

## Common Mechanism

Older cohorts received both the social contract of their era and capital gains. Younger cohorts face higher entry costs and wait for transfers that arrive late and unequally.

One land-value path into that pattern: society creates infrastructure → locations rise → owners gain equity → equity becomes collateral → more assets → advantage passes to the next generation. See `topics/taxing-activity-rewarding-scarcity.md`.

*(interpretation / causal-argument)*

## Who Benefits?

Older asset owners; families who can help early; heirs who receive housing security as membership.

*(interpretation)*

## Who Carries the Cost?

Younger adults without family capital; those asked to wait; societies that convert delayed adulthood into lower fertility, lower risk-taking, and exit.

*(interpretation / causal-argument)*

## Strongest Counterargument

Inheritance has always existed. Parents helping children is natural and good. Not all older people are rich; many younger people inherit eventually. Focusing on inheritance breeds resentment.

## My Response

The issue is scale and necessity. When inheritance or early family transfer becomes a normal requirement for adult stability—not a bonus—class is being rewritten through ownership.
