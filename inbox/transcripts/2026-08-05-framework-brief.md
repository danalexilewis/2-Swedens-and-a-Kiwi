Yes. The archive now needs to reflect the book you are actually writing:

An opinionated comparative economics book, supported by lived experience, plain-language explanation, and enough evidence to make the argument credible.

It is not an exposé, academic thesis, or work of investigative journalism. You do not need to prove every interpretation as if it were being submitted in court. You do need to distinguish facts from opinions, show readers where the important numbers come from, and be especially careful when making claims about identifiable people.

The core position

This should become the stable foundation of the repository:

Sweden and New Zealand are becoming capital-class societies: countries where a person’s security and opportunities increasingly depend less on what they earn and contribute, and more on what they and their family already own.

The shared failure is that political and economic management has allowed these advantages to compound across generations without meaningful limits or sufficient counterweights.

The result is unconstrained intergenerational inequality.

“Unconstrained” does not need to mean literally untaxed or entirely unregulated. It means:

The system does not contain strong enough mechanisms to stop wealth advantages from compounding, concentrating, and passing between generations faster than work can create equivalent security.

That is the result you are arguing toward.

You are not required to prove that every decision leading here was obviously wrong at the time. A fairer and stronger position is:

Some emergency decisions may have been reasonable. Some tradeoffs were genuinely difficult. But once the distributional effects became clear, both countries repeatedly failed to correct course.

The book is not primarily a trial of the past.

It is an argument against denial in the present.

The outcome the book is examining

The trajectory you are describing is:

Housing and financial assets rise faster than wages
            ↓
Existing owners gain wealth and borrowing power
            ↓
Tax advantages amplify the gains
            ↓
Wealth is transferred through families
            ↓
Housing security becomes increasingly inherited
            ↓
People without family capital delay ownership, children, and risk-taking
            ↓
Entrepreneurship and productivity suffer
            ↓
Young people leave for places with better opportunities
            ↓
Political power becomes increasingly aligned with older asset owners
            ↓
The system becomes even harder to reform

This is an argument map, not a claim that every arrow is already conclusively proven. Each connection becomes something the book explains, supports, challenges, and qualifies.

Your destination is:

The death of the broadly accessible home-owning middle class and the emergence of a society divided increasingly by capital ownership.

That does not necessarily mean the disappearance of all middle-income professionals. It means income alone becomes insufficient to secure what a middle-class income previously promised:

* A stable home
* Room for children
* Reasonable independence
* The ability to take professional or entrepreneurial risks
* A secure retirement
* A belief that the next generation can establish itself

Work pays for life.

Capital determines whether you can build security.

CAPITAL-CLASS-SOCIETY.md

This is a concept you need to define carefully because it may become one of the book’s major original formulations.

A working definition:

A capital-class society is one in which the decisive economic division is increasingly not between occupations or income groups, but between people who own appreciating assets and people who do not.

Characteristics include:

* Income covers consumption but struggles to create security.
* Property and financial assets compound faster than wages.
* Family wealth determines access to housing.
* Inheritance becomes a normal requirement for adult stability.
* Owners can borrow to acquire more ownership.
* Non-owners pay owners for access to housing and other essentials.
* Political policy is constrained by the need to protect asset values.
* Risk-taking becomes concentrated among people with family support.
* Economic mobility becomes increasingly dependent on family balance sheets.

This lets you distinguish a capital-class society from a society that merely has rich and poor people.

The central shift is:

Class becomes increasingly inherited through ownership rather than earned through work.

The initial topic files

housing-and-belonging.md

Central proposition:

Housing has changed from a broadly accessible foundation for middle-class security into an increasingly inherited or highly leveraged asset.

Questions include:

* Who can buy without parental help?
* How has ownership shifted by age?
* What does rental security look like?
* How much of income goes toward housing?
* What is the relationship between city property and rural property?
* Are people being encouraged to treat housing as retirement infrastructure?
* What happens when people cannot establish a stable home?

monetary-policy-and-assets.md

Central proposition:

Both countries managed consumer inflation while allowing the cost of economic membership—housing and other scarce assets—to rise dramatically.

This file should distinguish:

* Emergency intervention from long-term management
* Consumer prices from asset prices
* Interest-rate effects from supply constraints
* New Zealand’s housing-heavy financial system
* Sweden’s debt, property, and low-rate dynamics
* What was predictable at the time
* What became clear later
* What governments failed to correct

The book does not need to prove that quantitative easing should never have happened.

A stronger argument is:

Even where intervention was necessary, failing to manage its distributional consequences was a profound political and economic failure.

tax-preferences.md

Central proposition:

Both countries offer mechanisms that are formally available to everyone but whose greatest benefits flow to people with the most capital.

Main comparison:

* New Zealand property gains
* Swedish ISK and related capital treatment

Core line:

Equal access to a tax advantage does not produce equal benefit when access to capital is radically unequal.

The issue is not necessarily that these mechanisms exist.

It is that the benefit is insufficiently capped.

inheritance-and-generations.md

Central proposition:

The wealth created through decades of property and asset inflation is becoming intergenerational power.

This file should examine:

* Older homeowners gaining from asset inflation
* Parents inheriting from their own parents
* Adult children depending on family transfers
* Inheritance arriving too late
* Unequal family ability to help
* The difference between wealth on paper and liquid wealth
* The political power of older property-owning voters
* The cost of supporting long retirements
* Why younger generations are being asked to wait

Core line:

The old generation received the social contract and the capital gain. The next generation receives the invoice and waits for the inheritance.

productivity-and-risk.md

Central proposition:

Societies cannot demand innovation while making personal security dependent on avoiding risk.

This is where your choice between buying a house and funding a company belongs.

Examine:

* Mortgage eligibility rewarding predictable income
* Founders directing deposits into companies
* Families being unable to tolerate uncertain income
* Housing costs suppressing business formation
* The limited ability to experiment
* New Zealand’s scaling problem
* Sweden’s conformity and employment structures
* Productive investment versus passive asset holding

Core question:

How many businesses never exist because their potential founders cannot risk losing access to housing?

fertility-and-family.md

Central proposition:

Falling fertility may partly reflect societies that have made stable adulthood and family formation feel unsafe.

This must remain careful.

Your lived claim can be direct:

Had we possessed a stable home, room for children, and a secure enough income, our decision might have been different.

The broader causal claim requires evidence and qualification.

migration-and-brain-drain.md

Central proposition:

Exit may be rational for the individual while representing a profound national failure.

For New Zealand, Australia is an obvious comparison to investigate.

For Sweden, movement elsewhere in Europe is a research proposition that must be tested rather than assumed. The relevant questions are:

* Are younger or highly skilled Swedes leaving?
* Where are they going?
* Are they leaving permanently or temporarily?
* Are housing, wages, taxation, culture, or career opportunities material causes?
* Is Sweden suffering a brain drain, or a broader form of European labor mobility?
* Which groups are entering as others leave?

The chapter-level line is still powerful:

Politics treats the possible departure of a few multimillionaires as an emergency while treating the departure of ambitious young people as normal.

Your evidence standard

Create EVIDENCE-STANDARD.md with this principle:

The book is an opinion piece, but it is not careless.

Facts that need references

Always reference:

* Exact numbers
* Dates
* Tax rates
* Central-bank policies
* Housing data
* Migration data
* Fertility and employment statistics
* Company histories
* Public spending
* Legal rules
* Claims about identifiable people
* Statements about what an institution officially intended

Statements that do not need to pretend to be neutral facts

These can be written as your interpretation:

* The system is profoundly unfair.
* Political management has failed.
* Formal equality hides unequal capacity.
* The social contract is becoming an inheritance contract.
* Sweden can be suffocating of individuality.
* New Zealand wastes institutional capacity through political reversal.
* The home-owning middle class is dying.
* Both countries are becoming capital-class societies.

You should still support these arguments, but they are judgments. They do not need to be written as though an official body has certified them.

Causal claims need more care

Statements such as these need several supporting pieces:

* High housing costs reduce fertility.
* Asset inflation reduces productivity.
* Young people are leaving because they cannot buy homes.
* ISK materially increases wealth concentration.
* Quantitative easing created a particular person’s fortune.
* Political reversals caused a company to fail.

These can remain strong arguments, but the text should make the reasoning visible.

Predictions should be identified as predictions

For example:

If these trends continue, Sweden and New Zealand will become societies in which a middle-class income is insufficient to purchase a middle-class life.

That is more honest than presenting the future as an established fact.

Real people require the highest standard

Hans Wallenstam can illustrate the system, but he cannot carry the entire proof of it.

You can argue:

Wallenstam appears to have combined considerable business skill with an extraordinary monetary and property-market tailwind.

To make that argument, you need evidence about the company’s performance, leverage, interest costs, asset values, construction, and broader market conditions.

Avoid stating motives or attributing wealth to “pure luck.” The more interesting argument is that skill and structural advantage coexist.

Plain-language references

Use references to support the reader, not to make the book look academic.

A good target for most chapters is:

* A small number of load-bearing official statistics
* A few strong policy or economic sources
* One or two independent analyses
* Personal stories and observations
* A clear counterargument

You do not need a citation after every sentence.

Use simple Markdown footnotes while drafting:

New Zealand does not apply a comprehensive capital-gains tax to ordinary long-held housing gains.[^nz-capital-gains]
[^nz-capital-gains]: Source details and link.

A reference manager such as Zotero is sensible for collecting PDFs, links, reports, and citation details. Do not build your own reference-management application in Cursor.

The repository should contain short source notes, not every source document.
